# Do allergy shots actually work? (the evidence)

Short answer: **yes, and grass is one of the best-studied cases** — but "works" means *much
better*, not *cured*. That distinction is the whole honest core of this project. Full cited brief
with sources: `../../../compound-content/random/allergy-map/immunotherapy-evidence-brief.md`
(private working copy); the load-bearing citations are below.

## 1. It works for grass
Both delivery routes beat placebo in large pooled analyses:
- **Subcutaneous (shots / SCIT):** symptom reduction SMD **−0.73**, medication **−0.57** across
  51 trials. *(Calderon MA et al., Cochrane 2007, CD001936.)*
- **Sublingual (drops/tablets / SLIT):** symptom **−0.49**, medication **−0.32** across 60
  trials. *(Radulovic S et al., Cochrane 2010, CD002893.)*
- Grass tablets (e.g. Oralair) cut symptom scores **~34–36%** vs placebo.

Grass really is among the most-treatable aeroallergens — so a grass panel improving on shots is
the *expected* result, not luck.

## 2. The benefit lasts after you stop — if you did a full course
The landmark: after **3–4 years** of grass shots, people who then stopped **kept** their
improvement — "prolonged clinical remission." *(Durham SR et al., NEJM 1999;341:468–475.)* A
12-year cohort still showed benefit years out — **but** skin reactivity had partly crept back.
*(Eng PA et al., Allergy 2006;61:198–201.)* That partial return of reactivity is the honest
ceiling: durable ≠ permanent-and-total.

## 3. "Improves, doesn't cure" is exactly right
One large cohort reported **~77% remission** `[verify against the primary Kim et al. cohort
before print]` — meaning roughly 1 in 4 don't remit, a minority are non-responders, and full
permanent elimination is the exception, not the rule. **"Much better but never fully gone" is
textbook-typical.**

## 4. What a changed skin panel does — and doesn't — prove
Treated allergens can show smaller wheals over time (mechanism: IgG4 "blocking" antibodies plus a
damped late-phase skin response), while untreated ones persist. But skin/IgE reactivity
correlates only loosely with real symptoms — a quieter panel is **corroboration, not proof of
cure**, and it can rebound. So read my 2013→2023 chart as *strong supporting evidence* the shots
worked, not as a certificate that anything is gone for good.

## 5. Timeline & safety
3–6 months to build up, up to ~12 months to really feel it, a **3–5 year course** (the 3-year
minimum is what drives the lasting benefit). SCIT carries a small anaphylaxis risk (adrenaline
used in ~0.13% of injections, no deaths in the reviewed data); SLIT showed no anaphylaxis across
60 trials.

## How this maps to my case
Over ~13 years of shots, my trees, ragweed, molds, and dog fell off the panel and grass dropped a
lot but held on — squarely the "much better, never fully gone" pattern the evidence predicts.
When I stopped in 2026, I was told it resets and I'd have to rebuild `[FILL: exact guidance from
my allergist]`. The literature says a *completed* multi-year course tends to retain benefit after
stopping, with reactivity sometimes partly returning over years — individual mileage varies, and
this is a question for an allergist, not a webpage.

The point for this tool: even the treatment that works is **slow, expensive, and losable**. That
is precisely why knowing your *air* — a lever that costs nothing monthly — is worth having.
