# My Answer — where I can breathe (the worked example)

*The tool's first user is me. Grass-dominant panel (Bermuda/Bahia/Johnson/rye hard; weeds moderate;
ragweed/cedar/oak/elm/mold negative; ~13 yrs of shots, stopped 2026). Scored on the full model:
grass-season length × climate + planted/irrigated-turf multiplier + arid-Southwest weed/dust layer.
Then ground-truthed against every place I've actually lived and felt — which is the real test.*

## The green zero — my best air
- **Black Hills — Sundance WY / Rapid City SD** — my target, and my easiest air. High, dry, short
  May–July season, native pine (negative for me), almost no irrigated turf, no grass-seed ag.
  Sundance and Rapid City are ~50 mi apart — same clean Black Hills air. A cheaper, harsher-winter
  paradise with wildflower shoulders.
- **Coconino / Flagstaff AZ** — ~7,000 ft dry pine, grass sparse. On *pure* grass load, a hair
  cleaner than the Black Hills. But off my arc, cardiac-thin, ringed by arid desert-weed zones.

**Black Hills vs Coconino:** near-tie on air (Coconino edges it on altitude/dryness). Black Hills
wins the real decision — livable, on my Omaha→Wyoming path, near Rapid City + cardiac routes.

## The read
The model matches my body. Everything that wrecked me is grass-belt, irrigated-desert, or
year-round-no-reset. Everything I breathed easy in is high/dry/short-season. Austin was grass, not
cedar. Salt Lake looked dry but the lawns got me.

**Verdict:** Omaha → Wyoming runs straight down my best-air spine. No place is zero (grass survived
13 years of shots), but the Black Hills is as close as this country gets for me — and it's exactly
where I was already going.
